*Answered from stored clauses: searched the clauses of 99 of 99 contracts in scope. Counted as “Non-compete”: A party is barred from carrying on a competing business or competing activity of its own — a business, product line, territory or market — during or after the term: for example not selling or marketing competitive products, not acting as agent or distributor for competitive goods, not seeking competitive sales to the other's customers, or not operating in a territory reserved to another. A promise not to deal with, cooperate with or promote the other party's competitors (exclusive dealing) is exclusivity, not this; nor is not soliciting staff or customers, confidentiality, or an exclusive appointment that leaves the party's own competing activity free. Counted as “Exclusivity”: A party receives exclusive rights (an exclusive licence, appointment, territory or customer group) or commits to deal only with the other in the contract's subject — for example buying all its requirements from it, using only the other's product or technology, acquiring or dealing only through the other, not working with the other's competitors or with third parties in the subject, or working exclusively for the other. An "exclusive" court or jurisdiction, an exclusive remedy, or exclusive ownership of something is not this.*
