**Clear yes (10)**
- **Agape ATP – Supply Agreement (§12):** a right of first refusal. Agape, the customer, can buy the intellectual property for the Schedule A products before anyone else.
- **Embark.com – Co-Branding Agreement, Snap / United Airlines (§4.4):** headed "Right of First Refusal" but it works as a first negotiation. When the deal ends, Snap must negotiate a new sponsorship with United for 30 days before approaching third parties.
- **iVillage – Sponsorship Agreement (§4):** a right of first offer. Before iVillage signs a car-rental sponsor, it must give Hertz the chance on terms at least as good as anyone else's. Hertz has 5 business days. Note that Hertz is not a party to the contract.
- **King Pharmaceuticals – Promotion Agreement with Depomed (§13.2, also §13.1(a)):** an explicit right of first negotiation.
  - If Depomed wants to sell its U.S. rights to the product, King gets 30 days to evaluate and then 30 days of exclusive negotiation.
  - For six months afterwards, Depomed can't sell to a third party on materially better terms than King's last offer.
- **Leju Holdings – Internet Channel Cooperation Agreement with Baidu (§3.2):** when the agreement expires, Leju has a preferential right to continue the real-estate channel "with all conditions being equal". In effect this is a right of first refusal on renewal.
- **LinkPlus – Affiliate Agreement (§3.4):** as preferred manufacturer, LinkPlus has first right of refusal on every manufacturing request. Axiometric can go elsewhere only if LinkPlus doesn't respond within 30 days or can't offer competitive terms.
- **Remark Holdings – Content License Agreement, World Book / HSW International (§2.5):** an 18-month right of first refusal. HSWI can create any paid-subscription Chinese-language site using World Book content on terms equal to or better than a third party's.
- **Rocky Mountain Chocolate Factory – Strategic Alliance Agreement (§7.1, §7.2):** the company has both rights over shares the Farids Group wants to sell. It gets a right of first offer on Rule 144 block sales and a right of first refusal on transfers to a competitor.
- **Stamps.com – Sponsorship Agreement with Intuit (§12.4):** a right of first negotiation. Before selling ad or sponsorship space to a Stamps.com competitor, Intuit must negotiate in good faith with Stamps.com for 10 business days. After that it can't offer the space to others on better terms.
- **Todos Medical – Marketing and Reseller Agreement (§1.1):** the reseller has a right of first refusal to add any new Todos product to its exclusive distribution rights.

**Borderline (4)**
- **China Recycling Energy – Cooperation Agreement (Item Twelve):** Party A "has priority to develop further recycling energy projects" for Party B. It is a priority right, but there is no notice, matching or time period.
- **Coherus Biosciences – License and Development Agreement (§3.4):** if Bioeq wants to develop a new product for the U.S., it must notify Coherus and discuss in good faith whether it joins the licence. Several nearby sections are blacked out in the filed copy.
- **NetGrocer – Sponsorship Agreement (§7):** a duty to negotiate NetGrocer's place in future Excite programmes, with no priority over third parties.
- **Entrust – Strategic Alliance Agreement (§9.02):** Entrust must try to buy further equipment of one type from Northern Telecom first. There is no formal offer or matching procedure.
